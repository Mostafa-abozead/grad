# Executive Summary

Small and mid-sized factories and warehouses already own the cameras; what they lack is a way to act on what those cameras see. CCTV is reviewed after an incident, safety checks are manual and fragmented, and the evidence needed for audits, insurance, and regulatory reporting is incomplete. Commercial AI video-analytics platforms can close part of this gap, but they are closed, expensive, and built for large enterprises, while tag- or thermal-based alternatives add hardware, cost, and compliance burden. The result is a persistent safety blind spot: violations go unnoticed until an audit or an accident, and managers cannot answer basic questions about where risk is concentrated or why.

This project proposes an **AI-powered industrial safety and surveillance system** that turns existing CCTV/IP camera feeds into real-time, structured, actionable safety events — with **no additional hardware**. A shared computer-vision pipeline detects people, vehicles, and hazards; a config-driven rules engine converts those detections into safety events (restricted-zone intrusion, missing PPE, fire/smoke, proximity, worker location); and an output layer delivers the events through alerts, a risk dashboard, and an agentic-AI assistant. The architecture is deliberately **pluggable and API-driven**: every service is an independent unit integrated through a shared event schema, modeled on a generic, config-driven platform rather than a siloed point solution. The scope is camera-only — no thermal cameras and no radio/beacon personnel tagging — which keeps the system affordable for the target market and achievable within an academic project cycle.
