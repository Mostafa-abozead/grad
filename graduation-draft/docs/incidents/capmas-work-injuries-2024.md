# CAPMAS Work Injuries 2024: Extracted Evidence

## Purpose

This note extracts the most useful figures from the CAPMAS *Annual Bulletin of Work Injuries Statistics 2024* for the project's problem framing and sector prioritization. It is reference evidence, not a market-size study or product validation.

- **Source:** Central Agency for Public Mobilization and Statistics (CAPMAS), *Annual Bulletin of Work Injuries Statistics 2024*.
- **Local file:** [`docs/incidents/إحصاءات إصابات العمل _ 2024_compressed.pdf`](إحصاءات%20إصابات%20العمل%20_%202024_compressed.pdf)
- **Extraction date:** October 26, 2026.
- **Page references:** PDF page numbers, not the printed page numbers in the bulletin.
- **Rule used:** table values are treated as authoritative where the general-indicator prose conflicts with a table.

## Why This Matters for the BRD

- Egypt reported **8,091 work injuries** among **2,225,169 workers** in the covered establishments in 2024, a general injury rate of **363.6 per 100,000 workers**.
- **Manufacturing** accounts for **3,687 cases (45.6%)**, making it the main volume segment.
- **Construction** accounts for only **557 cases (6.9%)** but **22 of 48 deaths (45.8%)** and the highest fatal-injury rate (**37.1 per 100,000 workers**). It is the clearest severity segment.
- **Falls (32.1%)** and **collision with objects (27.6%)** together account for **59.7%** of cases. Upper and lower limbs account for **54.3%** of injured organs.
- **160 workers (2.0%)** recovered with a disability and **48 (0.6%)** died. This supports early-warning and prevention framing rather than post-incident reporting alone.
- The data is consistent with prioritizing PPE, falls, obstacle/collision, exclusion-zone and machine-proximity detection. It does not by itself prove demand for a specific product.

## Coverage and Method

The bulletin is compiled by CAPMAS from Ministry of Manpower notifications, with data collected in two six-month periods.

| Coverage item | Value |
|---|---:|
| Establishments | 7,596 |
| Government establishments | 1,335 (17.6%) |
| Public / public business establishments | 1,204 (15.9%) |
| Private establishments | 4,941 (65.0%) |
| Investment establishments | 116 (1.5%) |
| Workers covered | 2,225,169 |
| Injury cases | 8,091 |
| Absence days | 207,688 |

Scope notes:

- Private-sector coverage is **establishments with 50 or more workers**.
- The bulletin therefore represents a formal-sector snapshot, not a complete count of all workplace injuries or the informal sector.
- Rates and indicators follow the bulletin's stated ILO decent-work definitions.
- Percentages may not sum to 100 because of rounding. `=` means less than 0.05%; `0` means no statement.

## Data Quality Notes

The bulletin's general-indicator prose contains translation and ordering inconsistencies. Use the tables for citation.

- The English general indicators swap the 20–35 and 36–50 age numbers; Table 3 gives **20–35 = 3,204** and **36–50 = 3,703**.
- The general indicators attach 1,093 cases to health/social work; Table 5 assigns 1,093 to **accommodation and food services** and 696 to **health/social work**.
- The general indicators understate electrical current cases (10) and explosion cases (3); Tables 8 and 14 give **electrical current = 125** and **explosion = 10**.
- Total absence days appear as 207,688 in Tables 1 and 2 but 207,682 in Tables 5 and 24. This note uses **207,688**.
- Table 12 shows a `0.0` percentage for construction's "recovered with disability" despite 17 cases; the count is retained here.
- Arabic text extraction is unreliable because the PDF uses mixed direction and embedded fonts. English table values were used.

## Use in the BRD

- Use this as **Egypt-specific problem evidence** in the BRD context/background and as support for sector prioritization.
- Do not treat it as proof of willingness to pay, market size, or product demand.
- The strongest narrative is: **manufacturing for volume; construction for severity; falls, collisions and limb injuries for detection relevance**.
- Keep the existing scope unchanged. This evidence supports the current four-core direction but does not add new requirements.
